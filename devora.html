Devora

An AI pricing assistant that turns a full-day quote cycle into a two-minute, trustworthy estimate.

Role Product & UX Designer
Timeline 8 weeks
Platform Web widget + B2B SaaS dashboard
Overview

What is Devora?

Devora is an AI-guided pricing assistant that service businesses embed on their website. Instead of a static pricing page or a manual quote call, visitors answer a handful of quick questions in a chat-style widget and get a personalized estimate in under two minutes - while the business captures a qualified, genuine lead in the process.

How it works

A visitor answers a short set of questions about their project. AI drives the conversation and understands what they need; a deterministic rules engine - fully controlled by the business owner - calculates the price. The business gets an organized lead with full context; the visitor gets a real number, fast.

Why We Built This

The Bounce

Ads brought visitors to a pricing page. Session recordings showed most left within seconds - no scroll, no click, no form.

The Manual Cycle

Every business we spoke to described the same loop: ask the client's budget, get on a call, quote something "flexible" to avoid being wrong.

Tool Gap

Existing calculator tools (involve.me, Outgrow) are generic, spreadsheet-logic builders - not built around trust or lead quality for any one industry.

Our Opportunity

Conversational AI could ask the right questions fast - but only if paired with a pricing engine businesses could actually trust.

Framing the Challenge

For the Visitor

How might we give a fast, trustworthy price without forcing a call or a long form?

For the Business

How might we turn that same moment into a qualified, genuine lead - not a fake number typed just to see a price?
Journey

Two Versions, One Open Question

The single hardest tension in the product: gate the price behind a lead form, and you get more leads but a meaningful share are fake numbers typed just to see a result. Don't gate it, and the business loses its reason to exist. I designed two competing approaches and ran a small moderated test (8 participants, mix of past quote-seekers and IT founders) to see which one people actually behaved differently with.

V1 - Single Reveal

The direct approach

Answer six questions, enter your name and number, see the full price. Simple to build, simple to explain to a business owner.

What we heard

"I'd probably just put a fake number if I only wanted a ballpark," 3 of 8 participants said unprompted when shown this screen.

What we measured

5 of 8 said they'd hesitate or enter false details if only curious - the exact failure mode the gate was meant to prevent.

V2 - Progressive Reveal

A graduated-commitment approach borrowed from a simple idea

let curious visitors leave satisfied for free, and let only genuinely interested visitors reach the gate. After just 2-3 questions, a wide free range appears - no form required. Only visitors who want an exact number continue to the gate.

What we heard

"Oh, this already tells me if I'm in the right ballpark - I'd only bother going further if I was serious," one participant said.

What we measured

7 of 8 said they'd give real contact details at the second gate, since they'd already gotten value for free and had a reason to continue.

Neither version won outright - & that's the actual finding worth keeping. V1 is simpler to explain to a first-time business owner & ships faster. V2 produces higher-intent leads but adds a second decision point to design and build. Rather than force one answer, Devora ships both as an account-level setting - Single Reveal & Progressive Reveal - so each business can test which converts better for their own audience.

Architecture

Two Jobs, Two Systems

The riskiest assumption in a product like this is trusting an AI model to invent a price. Language models are excellent at conversation and terrible at staying numerically consistent - ask the same question twice, get two different answers. For a tool whose entire value is trust, that's disqualifying.

So the architecture splits cleanly in two: AI drives the conversation and understands what the visitor needs. A deterministic rules engine - simple, editable, numbers the business owner sets themselves - calculates the actual price. AI never touches the final number.

Conversation & Understanding

Handled by AI - reads free-text answers, asks natural follow-ups, drafts the human-toned WhatsApp handoff message.

Price Calculation

Handled by a rules engine - the business owner assigns a price to every answer; the same inputs always produce the same range.

Beyond that split, three principles guided every downstream decision:

Never ask what you can infer.

Onboarding pulls the business name, logo, industry, and brand color from a single URL - the owner confirms, doesn't fill out a form.

Every screen serves both sides.

Visitor and business goals are treated as equally important. A screen that helps one at the expense of the other gets redesigned.

Start simple, prove it, then branch.

Fixed question sequences over dynamic branching, single price ranges over complex formulas - complexity is earned with evidence, not assumed upfront.

Solution

The Visitor Experience

A single continuous chat, not a multi-page form. Every question is answerable in one tap - a free-text option is always available underneath, never the default path.

The Business Owner's Dashboard

Everything a non-technical business owner needs - questions, pricing logic, lead visibility - lives in one dashboard behind a single embed script. The developer's only job is a one-time copy-paste; every change after that belongs to the business team, not engineering.

Reflection

What Stuck With Me

Great design here wasn't about using AI everywhere — it was knowing where it earns its place: conversation, not the number a business stakes its reputation on. Testing both gate-timing versions instead of picking a winner upfront taught me more than either alone. This only became a real product once I stopped designing for my business and started designing for the pattern underneath it.
