One game for a 7-year-old and their grandparent.

A merge puzzle designed for children and players over 45 at the same time.

Built for everyone, designed for no one

Merge games are easy to download and easy to abandon. Most are tuned for one imagined player who is young, quick and plays in short bursts. Everyone else quietly drops off.

Tutorials nobody finishes

Children skip them and older players feel talked down to. The first board has to teach by being playable.

Targets made for young thumbs

Small tiles and tight timers quietly shut out the 45+ player.

A score with nowhere to go

Coins and stars are abstract. Nothing builds into something you can point at.

Difficulty that spikes

One unreadable round late in a world is where most players stop for good.

"Make the first move obvious and the fortieth move interesting."The rule I came back to every time

Every decision had to work for both

What the two audiences need
What it changed
Players over 45 often play at arm's length, one-handed, in poor light.
Tiles differ by shape as well as colour, on targets big enough to hit without aiming.
Children replay rounds. Older players rarely do.
Rounds stay replayable, but garden rewards drop only once. Replaying is for fun, not grinding.
Both groups quit at a round they can't read, not one that's hard.
Every board opens with at least one obvious merge.

One round, start to finish

One goal, in words, at the top

"Collect 3 Clovers", with moves left right beside it. No timer and no second currency. The board is the only thing asking for attention.

Merging up a tier

Each tier has its own silhouette, so a player who can't easily tell two greens apart still knows what they're holding.

Round complete, garden grows

The completion screen breaks down where every coin came from. Then everything you earned goes into the garden.

What stuck with me

Designing for a child and a grandparent at once forced every decision to hold up twice, and the game got simpler each time.

The instinct with a 45+ audience is to build a gentler copy with an easy mode off to one side. That would have made a worse game for everyone. What worked was making one game clear enough that nobody needs the concession.
